# Exact coupled-identity scan and modular rigidity for `p^2 q r` centers

This folder implements the "couple the four exact Gaussian quotient
identities" attack recommended in
[`../independent-2026-audit/README.md`](../independent-2026-audit/README.md)
for the sixteen `p^2 q r` classes left open by the monomial filters.

## The exact model

For a fixed class, the four offsets are **explicit monomials in the three
Gaussian primes** `pi, beta, gamma` over `p, q, r`; no projection to
valuations or heights is made.  A full `3 x 3` magic square of distinct
squares with center root `p^2 q r` realizes one of the 134 classes if and
only if its four offsets satisfy `{d_2, d_3} = {d_0 + d_1, |d_0 - d_1|}`
for that class's columns.  See [rigidity.md](rigidity.md), Section 1, for
the derivation and the exact sufficiency argument.

## Contents

- [coupled_p2qr_scan.cpp](coupled_p2qr_scan.cpp) - exact scanner over all
  134 classes: every ordered assignment of primes `= 1 mod 4` to the roles
  `(p, q, r)`, every class, all four generator choices modulo global
  conjugation, with optional proved-inequality pruning.  Detects full
  four-offset configurations (a hit is a genuine magic square of squares)
  and the weaker `111`/`211` three-offset events.  All arithmetic is exact
  in 128-bit unsigned integers with a self-test that re-embeds every
  sampled offset into `S_e`.
- [class_table.h](class_table.h) - generated by
  [gen_class_table.py](gen_class_table.py) from the two upstream
  classifiers; the generator verifies pairwise distinctness of the 134
  index matrices and the weighted row constraints.
- [torus_rigidity.cpp](torus_rigidity.cpp) / [torus_obstruction.py](torus_obstruction.py)
  - exhaustive modular-rigidity decision per class and auxiliary prime,
  through two independent implementations (C++ torus enumeration, Python
  torus parametrization) that agree on every modulus tested.
- [modular_obstruction.cpp](modular_obstruction.cpp) - a third
  implementation enumerating Gaussian units directly.
- [smooth_center_scan.cpp](smooth_center_scan.cpp) - exhaustive relation
  search over **every** center root whose split prime factors all lie
  below `sqrt(bound)`, covering all exponent patterns (`p q`, `p q r`,
  `p q r s`, `p^a q^b r^c`, ...) at once.  Relations only depend on the
  split part of `e` (inert primes scale all offsets by their square), so
  this height is stronger than a raw center-root bound; two-block centers
  are additionally excluded by the proved two-block theorem.  Validated
  counter-for-counter against an independent Python mirror.
- [rigidity.md](rigidity.md) - the rigidity lemma, the proved small-prime
  theorems (`l = 7, 11, 13`), the verified larger rigid primes, and the
  corollary.
- [test_coupled_p2qr_scan.py](test_coupled_p2qr_scan.py) - cross-validation
  suite: brute-force `S_e` agreement, table integrity, prune-count mirror,
  offset membership, self-test passthrough, and the rigidity criterion.

## Completed scans

All runs below found **zero** `111` relations, **zero** `211` relations,
**zero** full four-offset configurations, and no duplicate offsets.

| run | range | evaluations | machine |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| complete, all 134 classes, no filters | `p, q, r <= 10^4` (any `e <= 10^16`) | 120,468,735,744 | local, 14 threads |
| survivors only + proved inequalities | `p, q, r <= 5.5*10^4` (any `e <= 9.2*10^18`) | 20,221,435,336 | local, 14 threads |
| crosscheck complete | `p, q, r <= 2000` | 1,668,026,640 | dev, 4 threads (matches local bit for bit) |

The first line is unconditional: no magic square of squares with center
root `p^2 q r` exists for any three primes `= 1 mod 4` below `10^4`,
extending the repository's earlier `e <= 5*10^8` center-root bound by
eight orders of magnitude on this family.  The second line additionally
covers the sixteen filter survivors through `5.5*10^4` under the proved
block-balance inequalities of
[`../prime-support/three-block-p2qr.md`](../prime-support/three-block-p2qr.md),
with the prune path verified against an independent Python mirror.

The companion [smooth_center_scan.cpp](smooth_center_scan.cpp) enumerates
every center root up to `10^10` whose split prime factors are all at most
`sqrt(10^10) = 10^5` - 80,523,546 centers and 1,958,145,646 offsets - and
finds no `111` relation, no `211` relation, and no full configuration.
Because relations depend only on the split part of the center root (inert
primes scale every offset by their square), this is a stronger height than
a raw center bound and it covers all exponent patterns at once.

The modular rigidity results are documented in [rigidity.md](rigidity.md):
ten of the sixteen classes are rigid at explicit small auxiliary primes
(complete list through `l = 997`), forcing those primes to divide all four
offsets of any realization of any size; six classes (`ECE`/`EFC` roles)
are locally non-degenerate at every prime tested.

## Reproduce

```sh
clang++ -O3 -std=c++20 -Wall -Wextra -pedantic coupled_p2qr_scan.cpp \
  -o /tmp/coupled_p2qr_scan
/tmp/coupled_p2qr_scan --self-test
/tmp/coupled_p2qr_scan --primes=10000            # complete scan
/tmp/coupled_p2qr_scan --primes=55000 --survivors-only --prune
python3 -m unittest -v test_coupled_p2qr_scan.py
```

No hit of any kind was found; a positive `FULL` event would be a
counterexample to the open problem, and a positive `111` or `211` event
would be the first known three-offset relation for a `p^2 q r` center.
